## 0002.1 — measurement harness

`crates/engine_stress` runs seven deterministic scenarios and records counters
that are identical on every machine. Counters are committed to
`fixtures/stress/baseline.json` and asserted in CI; timings are printed, never
committed, never asserted.

Byte accounting was added to the engine to support it: `Volume::cell_bytes`,
`Volume::palette_bytes`, `MeshData::cpu_bytes`, and mesh bytes/indices on
`CacheStats`.

### Baseline, as first recorded

Measured in release on a 4-core cloud container. Counters are exact and asserted;
timings are indicative only.

| scenario | volumes | cells | faces | quads | merge | cell+palette | mesh | build ms | median edit ms |
|----------|--------:|------:|------:|------:|------:|-------------:|-----:|---------:|---------------:|
| dense_solid | 64 | 262,144 | 24,576 | 96 | 256.0x | 512 KiB | 20 KiB | 54.7 | — |
| sparse_terrain | 111 | 259,044 | 49,448 | 9,504 | 5.2x | 889 KiB | 2.0 MiB | 88.1 | — |
| checker | 8 | 16,384 | 98,304 | 98,304 | 1.0x | 64 KiB | 20.2 MiB | 21.4 | — |
| material_stress | 8 | 32,768 | 6,144 | 6,144 | 1.0x | 66 KiB | 1.3 MiB | 5.4 | — |
| edit_storm | 8 | 32,646 | 6,844 | 674 | 10.2x | 64 KiB | 142 KiB | 4.7 | 0.445 |
| boundary_storm | 8 | 32,644 | 6,708 | 696 | 9.6x | 64 KiB | 147 KiB | 4.6 | 0.932 |
| travel_strip | 110 | 249,780 | 55,666 | 8,856 | 6.3x | 881 KiB | 1.8 MiB | 113.5 | — |

### What the first baseline already settles

**Mesh data dominates, and by how much depends entirely on the shape.** On
realistic terrain mesh is ~2.3x cell storage — close to the 2:1 estimated from
the DROP 0001 demo scene, so that estimate holds up. But the spread is enormous:
a solid world costs *more to store than to draw* (20 KiB of mesh against 512 KiB
of cells), while the checker case inverts it completely at **316x** — 20.2 MiB of
mesh over 64 KiB of cells, or 1,296 bytes of mesh per occupied cell.

This matters for the residency budget in 0002.10: a budget derived from cell
counts would be wrong by two orders of magnitude depending on terrain shape.
**Budget on mesh bytes, measured, not on cell or chunk counts.**

**Merging is not uniformly valuable.** 256x on solid, 5-6x on terrain, and
exactly 1.0x on both checker and material_stress. The material_stress result is
the interesting one: the shape is a plain solid block whose shell is only 6,144
faces, but scattered materials block every merge. Material variety costs as much
as geometric complexity.

**Boundary edits cost about twice an interior edit at the median, and far more at
the tail** — 0.932 ms against 0.445 ms median, with a 5.9 ms worst case against
0.6 ms. That is the neighbour-invalidation rule doing exactly what it should, and
it is a measured argument for the scheduler in 0002.8 needing to coalesce.

**Nothing here is a frame-rate claim.** These are CPU-side compile measurements on
a software-rendering container.

## 0002.2 — spatial-role separation

The five conflated ideas now have separate names and separate types.

| was | is | role |
|-----|----|------|
| `ChunkPos` | `VolumePos` | the 16³ storage leaf |
| — | `RegionPos` | residency and persistence, 8×8×8 volumes |
| — | `RenderSectionId` | a unit of compiled geometry |
| `ChunkMeshCache` | `SectionMeshCache` | keyed by section, not by volume |
| `Chunk` struct | *deleted* | it was a wrapper that **was** a volume |

`SectionGrid` maps volumes onto sections. It is one-to-one today, which is a fine
implementation and a terrible assumption — so every caller goes through it rather
than using a volume address as a section address. `SectionGrid::new(2)` already
works and is tested: eight volumes become one submission, with the world model
untouched.

Grouping **concatenates** quads, it never merges across a volume boundary. The
tests assert that explicitly: a grouped section and eight separate sections
produce the *same quad count* and the *same unit faces*, differing only in how
many submissions the renderer receives. Cross-volume merging would change the
geometry and needs its own correctness story, so it stays out of this drop.

The world now reports dirty **volumes** and the cache maps them to sections,
deduplicating as it goes — four dirty volumes sharing a section cause one rebuild,
not four. That deduplication is what makes a larger section grid affordable later.

### The refactor changed no behaviour, and that is checkable

Every stress counter is byte-identical to the baseline recorded in 0002.1. That is
the harness earning its keep one pass after being built: a restructure this broad
would otherwise have been argued about rather than verified.

115 tests pass, including nine new spatial tests in `engine_core` and four new
section-separation tests in `engine_geometry`.

### Deliberately not done here

`RegionPos` exists as an address; the region *container*, ownership of volumes and
load-order independence are 0002.4. The meshers still compile one volume at a
time — generalising them to arbitrary cell bounds is what cross-volume merging
would need, and it is out of scope.

## 0002.3 — floating render origin

World coordinates stay integer and authoritative; the world never moves. Rendering
converts `global − render origin → small local → f32`, with the origin following
the camera in region-aligned steps.

| piece | where |
|-------|-------|
| `GlobalPos` — authoritative f64 cell-space position | `engine_core::origin` |
| `RenderOrigin` — aligned anchor, rebase with hysteresis | `engine_core::origin` |
| section-local vertex positions | `MeshData::origin` |
| camera position, transform derived | `apps/sandbox/src/camera.rs` |

### Why mesh positions became section-local here

If vertices carried global positions, an origin shift would mean rebuilding every
mesh. Emitting them relative to the owning section's minimum cell means a rebase
costs **one transform update per section and no mesh rebuilds at all**. It also
keeps every vertex coordinate inside a section's own extent, so `f32` precision is
constant no matter how far the player has travelled.

This was listed as a 0002.12 memory candidate. It moved forward because floating
origin needs it structurally, not because it saves bytes.

### Constants

`ORIGIN_ALIGN_CELLS` is one region (128 cells), so an anchor never splits a
residency unit. `ORIGIN_REBASE_DISTANCE_CELLS` is two regions, which gives the
same hysteresis idea the streaming radii will use: hovering on a boundary does not
rebase every frame.

### Verified

Nine unit tests cover alignment below zero, round trips across zero, rebasing
preserving every relative position, edits resolving to the same cell either side
of a shift, and render coordinates staying under 1,000 at four million cells out.

Also verified in the running app: flying ~700 cells moved the camera to
`-358 -225 -420` with the origin following to `-256 -256 -384` — every component a
multiple of 128, and the camera within 102 cells of its anchor, so the hysteresis
held. No visual discontinuity, no crash.

A latent bug went with it: `raycast` previously built its DDA from absolute `f32`
coordinates, which quietly loses sub-cell accuracy far from the origin. It now
takes a `GlobalPos` and converts only the fraction within the start cell.

Stress counters are unchanged again — section-local positions alter vertex values,
not vertex counts.

## 0002.4 — region layer

Storage is now two levels: the world owns `Region`s, a region owns the 16³ volumes
inside it. Loading or evicting a region is one map insert or removal rather than
512 of them, which is the whole reason the tier exists.

`World::insert_region` and `World::remove_region` are the streaming primitives.
Both mark the right things dirty: every volume that arrives or departs, **and**
every volume in a neighbouring region that touched the seam — because faces that
were hidden against a loaded neighbour become exposed when it goes away. That is
the 0002.2 neighbour rule extended across a region boundary, and it is tested in
both directions.

### Two different dirtinesses

They are easy to conflate and expensive to confuse:

| dirty | means | tracked by |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| volume | geometry needs rebuilding | `World::take_dirty` |
| region | has unsaved edits | `Region::is_dirty` |

Losing the second is how a streaming engine silently eats a player's work, so a
region loaded from disk starts clean and any edit marks it unsaved.

### Iteration order, made explicit

Volume iteration is now **region-major**, which is deterministic but not the same
as globally ascending volume order. The save format used to lean on the world's
iteration order being sorted; it now sorts explicitly via `World::volumes_sorted`.
The committed-fixture test proves the output is byte-identical to before.

### A semantic that got clearer

An emptied volume is now indistinguishable from one that never existed —
`volume_count` counts populated volumes only. Its storage genuinely lingers until
compaction, which `allocated_volume_count` reports and `footprint` still charges
for, because that memory really is still allocated.

### Verified

Thirteen region tests: tiling without gaps or overlap across region seams,
negative coordinates, boundary cells, eviction round-tripping exactly, dirty-region
tracking, ascending iteration, residency accounting, and the one that matters most
for streaming — **world contents are identical whatever order regions are loaded
in**, checked forwards, backwards and interleaved.

148 tests pass. The stress baseline moved by exactly one field, `resident_regions`,
from 0 to its real value; every other counter is unchanged, so the restructure
again altered no behaviour. `travel_strip` reports 4 regions for its 32-volume
length, as it should.

## 0002.5 — persistence v2

A sharded directory: `world.json` manifest plus `regions/r.<x>.<y>.<z>.json`. See
[`native-world-format.md`](native-world-format.md) for the schema.

The point is not tidiness. v1 held the whole world in one file, so loading a
single region meant parsing everything and saving one edit meant rewriting
everything. `load_region` and `save_region` now touch exactly one shard — the
streaming primitive — and `save_dirty_regions` writes only regions with unsaved
edits and marks them clean, which is what makes "save before evict" affordable.

The manifest adds what v1 had nowhere to put: world id, name, spawn, and a
placeholder for generator settings. `WorldMeta` is deliberately separate from
`World`, so "do these two worlds hold the same geometry?" stays a question about
geometry.

### Failure modes are loud

A world that silently loses a region is worse than one that refuses to load. Four
distinct errors, each tested: a listed shard missing from disk, a shard whose
`pos` disagrees with the name it was loaded as, a volume that does not belong to
the region claiming it, and a directory with no manifest.

### Verified

Twenty-one v2 tests alongside the twenty v1 tests, which all still pass
unchanged. Coverage: round trips, canonical byte-identical output, edit history
not affecting bytes, single-region load, load-order independence through disk,
dirty-only saves, **edit → save → evict → reload preserving the edit exactly**,
every failure mode above, forward tolerance of unknown keys, v1 migration
including the committed DROP 0001 fixture, and a committed v2 fixture that must
stay canonical.

## Next smallest implementation step

0002.6 — the residency state machine. Regions can now be loaded, saved and
evicted, but nothing tracks *where each one is* in that lifecycle. Give them
explicit states — Unloaded, Requested, Loading, Resident, Meshing, Ready,
EvictRequested, Saving — so that "is this safe to evict?" and "is a load already
in flight?" are answerable rather than inferred from a pile of booleans. Testable
with no Bevy and no GPU.
