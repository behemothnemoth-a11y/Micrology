# DROP 0006 — Core Destruction: the Fracture Model

Status: **in progress**

DROP 0006 is about one question, asked properly for the first time:

> what happens *inside* one solid material when one impact hits it?

Everything before this drop could answer only "some cells reached a threshold and
stopped existing". The pipeline that did it is correct, proven and staying — but
it has no state between *intact* and *absent*, so an impact could only ever
delete a shape, and the shape it deleted was the shape of the event. That is why
destruction still read as

```text
damage -> cells disappear -> connectivity -> clean fragment falls
```

even though every stage of it works.

This drop is not about making destruction prettier. It is about building the
**real fracture state** that later visuals, materials, fragments and collapse can
all derive from. A crack has to exist as engine data before it can be rendered,
persisted, streamed, re-broken or made to differ between wood and steel.

**One material, on purpose.** The whole drop develops against a single
homogeneous baseline solid. Material diversity is DROP 0005's subject and it is
deliberately switched off here: with five materials in the fixture, a
fracture-model problem and a tuning problem look identical.

> DROP 0005 is not finished — 0005.8, participation and fantasy acceptance,
> remains open, as does closing out the DROP 5 capture work. Taking the core
> fracture model first is a deliberate reordering, not an omission: the
> destruction model is what everything else in the drop ends up expressed in, and
> getting it wrong later is more expensive than getting 0005.8 late.

## 0006.0 — baseline fracture model and one impact

**Status: complete**

Adds `engine_destruction::fracture`: sparse persistent fracture state and a
deterministic reference impact evaluator. One homogeneous wall, one localised
impact, repeated — and the questions are where the energy went, what weakened,
what actually failed, and what is still standing.

### Where fracture sits, and why it is not anywhere else

Fracture **consumes `DamageEvent` directly, beside the scalar path.** Three
placements were on the table and two of them do not work:

- **After `DamageWork`** does not work. `DamageWork` is `{event, target, amount}`.
  By the time an event has become work the geometry is gone: no impact position,
  no direction, no distance. A model whose whole subject is *where the energy
  went* cannot be built on a representation that threw the geometry away.
- **Inside `ProgressiveDamageStore`** does not work either. That store is one
  scalar per cell and deletes its record at threshold, which is the right
  contract for accumulated applied damage and the wrong one for a crack. Merging
  them would also put one accumulator under two different threshold policies — a
  weapon hit and an impact are not the same question about a cell.
- **Beside, from the same event** works, because `DamageEvent` already carries
  `source_world`, `impulse_world` and the affected volume: exactly the three
  things a fracture model needs and the three things `DamageWork` discards.

```text
             DamageEvent
                  |
    +-------------+--------------+
    |                            |
 DamageWork                 FractureImpact
    |                            |
 ProgressiveDamageStore      FractureState   (cell energy + bond integrity)
    |                            |
    +-------------+--------------+
                  |
            failed cells
                  |
       WorldEditBatch -> connectivity -> Fragment
```

`DamageEvent`, `DamageWork` and `ProgressiveDamageStore` are **unchanged**. Not
one line of the DROP 0004 damage contract moved. A host may run both paths from
one event, or either alone, and neither knows the other exists.

### Bonds, and the invariant this does not touch

State is sparse and held two ways:

- per **cell**, accumulated absorbed energy, which destroys the cell outright at
  the profile's crush threshold;
- per **bond** — one face between two occupied cells — a remaining integrity that
  falls as the bond is loaded and reaches zero when it breaks.

A bond representation is right here because Micrology's structural topology is
*already* face-connected, so a crack has somewhere natural to live: two cells
stay present while the connection between them stops existing. That is the state
everything later wants — an irregular break surface, a weak region the next hit
concentrates into, a plug that is loose before it is gone.

**Occupancy connectivity remains the only topological oracle.** `classify_from_roots`
is untouched and means exactly what it did. A broken bond is a crack, not a cut,
and nothing in the fracture module detaches anything. Fracture-aware
connectivity — treating a broken bond as a cut when deciding what is held up — is
0006.1, and when it arrives it sits *beside* the exact occupancy classifier
rather than replacing it. Weakening one of the strongest invariants in the engine
to make cracking easier would be a bad trade at any price.

In this pass a bond breaking reaches geometry by exactly one route: **a cell all
of whose bonds have broken is held by nothing, and fails.** That failure is an
ordinary `DamageTarget`, so it flows through `static_failure_batch`,
`WorldEditBatch`, connectivity and the fragment pipeline without any of them
learning a new concept.

### A bond is named once

`BondKey` stores the **lower** cell plus the axis, with private fields and
normalising constructors. The obvious `(cell, FaceDir)` key names every face
twice, and two aliases of one crack accumulate damage separately — so a bond
would break at twice the load its capacity says. Derived ordering is
`(lower, axis)`, which is what makes `BTreeMap` output canonical.

### The model

Six numbers describe the baseline solid, and each has exactly one job:

| field | job |
|---|---|
| `crush` | accumulated absorbed energy that destroys a cell |
| `tensile` | bond capacity pulled apart — lowest |
| `compressive` | bond capacity driven together — highest |
| `shear` | bond capacity across the face |
| `toughness_milli` | share of local energy soaked up rather than turned into cracks |
| `density_milli` | mass of one cell; carried as authored data, read by nothing here |

Toughness is used **once**, as the complement of brittleness in bond loading.
Using it again anywhere would make the material quietly twice as durable as its
numbers say — the same mistake `engine_mechanics` records for toughness in
applied damage, in the same shape. Density is deliberately inert: fragment mass
already has its own reference policy, and giving the number a second job would be
two models of one quantity.

An impact deposits energy, and bonds are loaded by the **gradient** of that
deposit:

1. **Walk, do not sample.** The propagation is a bounded face walk over occupied
   cells from a small seed box around the impact origin. Walking is what puts
   local geometry in the answer: energy cannot cross a void, and a cell reachable
   only by a long detour is out of range even when it is close. Attenuation is by
   straight-line distance, because attenuating by face-walk distance makes a
   solid's damage field octahedral — graph distance in a solid *is* Manhattan
   distance, and it shows.
2. **Direction matters.** A cell's deposit is raised up to 1.6x facing the
   impulse and cut to 0.4x facing away. This is the single thing that stops the
   result being a sphere.
3. **Free surfaces spall.** A cell with an exposed face has nowhere to pass the
   energy on, so it keeps what would have travelled onward. This is why the back
   of a struck wall breaks out rather than only the face that was hit, and it is
   the main source of an irregular break surface rather than a clean shell.
4. **A bond is loaded by the difference across it.** Uniform energy on both sides
   moves two cells together; only a gradient drives them apart. So the biggest
   loads land at the *edge* of the damaged region rather than at its hottest
   point, which is why a plug comes loose instead of a sphere vanishing.
5. **Mode comes from geometry, not from authored data.** The cell the wave
   reaches first is upstream. Upstream absorbing more means it is driven into the
   one ahead: compression. Downstream absorbing more means it is running away:
   tension. Capacity is blended between the axial mode and shear by how well the
   bond's axis aligns with the impulse, so there is no threshold cliff. One rule,
   and it makes the struck face crush while the back face — raised by the
   free-surface bonus — tears off in tension, where the material is weakest.
6. **Damage concentrates at a crack tip.** A bond beside already-broken bonds
   carries more of the next hit, capped so that a bond surrounded by damage
   cannot break to an arbitrarily small load. This is what makes a repeated hit
   *grow* the existing damage instead of re-damaging a fresh ring, and it is the
   evolving weak region the design asked for.
7. **There is a fatigue floor.** A load below 2% of bond integrity is not
   recorded. Two things come out of that and both are wanted: the state stays
   proportional to the damage rather than to the reach, and an arbitrarily weak
   hit repeated forever never breaks anything — which is what real materials do.

### Determinism

The model is integer. `DamageEvent` carries `f64` world coordinates, so there is
exactly **one** quantisation step — `FractureImpact::from_event` — and everything
downstream is `i64`/`i128` with saturating conversions. Cell centres never go
through floating point at all: a cell address is an `i32`, so its centre in
milli-cells is `x * 1000 + 500` exactly. Output is canonicalised through
`BTreeMap`, so traversal order is not observable, and the deltas come out
ascending by cell and by bond key.

`Milli` was not reused. It lives in `engine_mechanics`, which is *downstream* of
`engine_destruction`, and fracture state has to be storable beside fragments
whether or not a world opted into mechanics.

### Conservative under everything

- **Unknown is not empty.** A non-resident neighbour *within the impact's reach*
  yields `Indeterminate` and the regions needed. Beyond the reach it cannot
  change the answer, so it is not reported — being conservative about the unknown
  is not the same as being paralysed by it, and reporting every region boundary
  would defer every impact near one for nothing.
- **A spent budget is not destruction.** Work ceilings yield `Deferred`, which
  carries no deltas and can mutate nothing.
- **Memory ceilings refuse the whole transaction.** There is no half-applied
  fracture state, and a refusal does not advance the revision.
- **Work and byte budgets are separate**, as everywhere else: cells walked and
  bonds loaded are the work bounds; cell entries and bond entries are the memory
  bounds.
- **Async results are stale-checked.** An evaluation carries the state `Revision`
  it was computed against and is refused if the state has moved, so a result
  computed on a worker thread cannot be committed onto state that changed under
  it.
- **An anchored cell is never failed by losing its bonds.** Losing bonds is a
  structural inference, and an anchor is the author's statement that something is
  held up regardless — the same rule 0005.6 arrived at. It can still be crushed,
  because that is applied damage and applied damage has never respected anchors.
- **Stale state is forgotten, not inherited.** `forget_cell` drops a cell's
  record and every bond touching it, for when geometry disappears by a path
  fracture did not drive. A bond to a cell that is gone is a hole, not a crack.

### One material, by construction

`BaselineFracturePolicy` applies one profile to every material because it holds
one profile and no table. Material diversity is not merely unused here, it is
**unexpressible**. Making fracture material-aware later is the move DROP 0005
already made for damage: a new `FracturePolicy` implementation in
`engine_mechanics`, with nothing in this module changing.

### The permanent fixture: the Micrology destruction wall

32 cells wide, 18 tall, 5 thick, on an anchored footing wider than itself, one
material throughout. Exact dimensions are not sacred; large enough to form
meaningful chunks and small enough to read at a glance is.

### What the model actually does

The same hit on the same spot, seven times, energy 900, reach 7:

| hit | cracks opened | cells removed | damaged cells | loaded bonds | broken bonds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 0 | 497 | 1840 | 0 |
| 2 | 4 | 1 | 496 | 1835 | 4 |
| 3 | 28 | 0 | 496 | 1835 | 32 |
| 4 | 184 | 5 | 491 | 1814 | 208 |
| 5 | 308 | 4 | 487 | 1814 | 508 |
| 6 | 201 | 33 | 454 | 1721 | 621 |
| 7 | 180 | 21 | 433 | 1652 | 756 |

The first hit leaves the wall standing and measurably weaker, which is the thing
the old pipeline could not express. Seven hits take 64 of 3132 cells — about 2% —
and what they take is not a sphere:

```text
struck face (z=2)              mid-wall (z=1, z=0)       back (z=-1)
##############.###.#########   ###############...####    #########.####
#############..###..########   ###############...####    ##############
############.........#######   ###############...####    ##############
##############.....#########
############.........#######
#############..###..########
##############.###.#########
```

A ragged, lobed crater at the face it was hit on, narrowing to a 3x3 channel
through the middle, with a single cell punched out one layer short of
perforating. The lobes are the free-surface rule doing its work; a spherical mask
has no reason to produce them.

The crack field reaches considerably further than the hole: **hole half-width 4
cells, crack half-width 7** — so roughly three times the area of the wall is
present-and-weakened as is gone, and the next hit anywhere inside that will find
it.

Cost for one hit of that size: 817 cells walked, 1960 bonds loaded. Both are
bounded and both are reported; neither is presented as a frame-rate claim.

`cargo run -p engine_destruction --example fracture_wall` prints this table, the
spreads and every slice through the wall, so none of it has to be taken on trust
— and it needs no GPU.

### Acceptance

24 / 24 focused tests, including:

- **Test A** — one weak hit damages the wall without removing a single cell, and
  what it leaves is not a spherical mask: a cell downstream of the impulse
  absorbs strictly more than one the same distance upstream.
- **Test B** — repeating the same hit builds on the previous state rather than
  resetting it, opens the first cracks on the second hit, never un-breaks a
  crack, and eventually produces local failure.
- cracks reach further than the hole;
- the struck face is compressed while the far side is pulled apart;
- the same sequence replays to identical state and identical surviving geometry;
- a stale evaluation, an exhausted work budget and an exhausted entry budget each
  change nothing;
- unknown space within reach is indeterminate, and unknown space beyond it is not;
- energy cannot cross a void to reach a separate slab;
- an anchored cell is never failed by losing its bonds;
- a hit below the fatigue floor never breaks anything however often it repeats.

Every existing test still passes: the full `cargo test` suite is green and
nothing outside the new module and its registration changed in any engine crate.

### The diagnostic

`MICROLOGY_FRACTURE_DEMO=1` replaces the scene with the destruction wall, fixes
the camera square on to the struck face, holds the intact wall, then fires the
same impact seven times and holds the result.

The overlay draws the fracture state itself, not an impression of it: a
cross-marked red quad on every face whose bond has broken, an amber cross in
every cell past a quarter of its crush threshold sized and coloured by how close
it is, and a white marker at the impact origin. It draws with a negative depth
bias on purpose — fracture state lives *inside* a solid wall, and a diagnostic
that respected depth would show almost none of it. `H` hides the overlay and the
text together, for capture. The status block reports the real sparse-state
counters, the work the last impact cost, and how many impacts were held by a
budget or by unknown space.

No particles. Nothing in the picture exists that is not in `FractureState`.

### What is deliberately not in this pass

- **Fracture-aware connectivity.** Broken bonds do not yet decide what is held
  up, so a region the cracks have genuinely separated still hangs together until
  cells are removed. This is the single biggest thing 0006.0 leaves on the table
  and it is 0006.1.
- **Region-granular persistence.** `forget_cell` and `forget_space` keep the
  state honest when geometry leaves, but there is no `take_static_region` /
  restore pair yet, so fracture state does not travel with a streamed region.
- **Fragment-local fracture.** The API is space-aware throughout — a
  `FractureScene` can be a fragment — but DROP 0004.8's fragment re-damage path
  still runs on the scalar model, and folding it into this one is a later
  section.
- **Material diversity, LOD, chain collapse, visual crack geometry.** Not now,
  and not by accident.

## 0006.1 — fracture-aware connectivity, beside the oracle

**Status: complete**

Adds `engine_destruction::fracture_connectivity`: a second structural classifier
that treats a broken bond as a cut, and the benchmark runner that executes the
directional cases against it.

### The thing 0006.0 could not do

A plug could be completely ringed by broken bonds and stay standing, because the
only route from a broken bond to geometry was *a cell that has lost every bond*.
Separating anything therefore still needed the engine to delete a shell of cells
first. That artificial step is gone: a crack is now a cut for the purpose of
asking what is held up.

Six cells are enough to show it. A one-wide column on an anchored base, one bond
broken at mid-height, **zero cells removed** — and the three cells above the
crack come back as one loose component while the occupancy oracle still reports
the whole column supported. That disagreement is not a bug to reconcile; it is the
capability.

### Two classifiers, and why there are two

`classify_from_roots` is untouched and remains the permanent exact topological
oracle. The fracture-aware walk is a **separate implementation** that shares none
of its machinery — the same relationship `ExactCompiler` has with
`GreedyCompiler`, for the same reason: a search that shared code with the thing it
validates could not validate it.

The agreement is asserted rather than assumed. With no bond broken, the new
classifier must reproduce the oracle's components, classifications, visited-cell
count and merged-root count **exactly**, and that is tested on the destruction
wall, a floating slab, a wall with a hole punched through it, an anchored wall
beside an unprofiled floater, edge-only contact, a wall beside an unloaded
region, and under an exhausted budget. Seven shapes, one assertion: if these ever
disagree on an un-cracked world, the new one is wrong.

### The difference is named, not smoothed over

`separation_from_cracks` labels every loose component with why it is loose:

| cause | meaning |
|---|---|
| `SeparationCause::Occupancy` | material was removed; ordinary DROP 0003 destruction |
| `SeparationCause::Cracks` | nothing was deleted; only the broken bonds separate it |

The oracle is consulted **once**, through its own multi-root entry point, and
every fracture-detached component is labelled from the occupancy component its
lowest cell landed in. Asking per component would give the same answer at N times
the cost, because the oracle never exits early.

### Bounded, incremental roots

`separation_roots` turns a fracture transaction into the cells worth asking
about: the endpoints of every bond that broke, plus the face neighbours of every
cell that failed — the same candidates the occupancy path already gets from
`EditOutcome::structural_candidates`, for the same reason. There are exactly two
ways a component can newly come loose and this covers both; it was measured
missing the second before the failure neighbours were added.

Work is bounded by the damage rather than by the world, and the query is
**incremental**: a transaction that broke nothing and removed nothing finds
nothing newly loose, including components an earlier transaction freed that the
caller has not yet acted on. Measured against classifying every cell in the wall
after every hit, the two agree component-for-component on every step once the
caller acts on what it is told.

### Nothing new removes anything

Detached components go through `cracked_structure_result` into the **existing**
`detach_if` transaction: the same staleness check, the same atomic admission, the
same deterministic fragment identity, the same `WorldEditBatch`. A refusing
admission policy changes nothing and does not consume a fragment id. A stale
result is refused. An unsettled one detaches nothing. There is no second
detachment mechanism, because a second way to destroy something is a second thing
to keep correct.

### Conservative in the same three ways, plus one

- **Unknown beats broken.** Residency is checked *before* the bond gate. A bond
  record can outlive the cell on the far side of it when a region is evicted
  rather than carved, so a broken bond is never licence to conclude anything
  about space the engine cannot see.
- **A spent budget is `Deferred`** and separates nothing.
- **An inconclusive cross-check downgrades the component.** If the oracle cannot
  say whether a piece was *already* loose, the reason is missing, so the piece
  stops being actionable rather than being guessed at.
- **A detached chunk takes its records with it.** `fracture_state_leaving_with`
  names the state a detachment makes meaningless, and a test walks every
  surviving bond to prove none of them describes a cell that has left.

### The benchmark runs now

`engine_stress::destruction_runner` executes the cases the engine can answer,
reading each stimulus from the **committed pack** through `fracture_hit_toward`
rather than from a hand-written event — so a measurement cannot drift away from
the protocol it claims to follow. The generic builder is pinned by test to
reproduce the canonical weak hit byte for byte, so 0006.0's anchor number is
still the same number.

`--run` prints measured results; `--write-results` / `--check-results` keep
`fixtures/destruction/benchmark-results.json` canonical, so an improvement or a
regression is a reviewable diff. Acceptance is **checked**: the pack's
`Required`/`Forbidden` requirements become assertions, and a case a later section
owns reports `null` rather than a passing zero.

Measured, one repetition unless the pack says otherwise:

| case | cells walked | broken bonds | failed cells | freed by cracks | freed by deletion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `weak_center_hit` | 817 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `repeated_center_hits` (3x) | 2450 | 32 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| `strong_center_hit` | 817 | 184 | 5 | 0 | 0 |
| `edge_hit` | 516 | 39 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| `angled_hit` | 842 | 59 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Controls, run rather than asserted from intuition:

- **cumulative** — three repetitions leave 32 broken bonds against **0** for one.
- **edge** — the identical hit at the wall centre walks 817 cells, loads 1932
  bonds and breaks 16; at the free edge it walks 516, loads 1207 and breaks
  **39**. Less material to reach, and more of it breaks, because free surfaces
  keep the energy that would have travelled onward.
- **angled** — the same cell and energy struck square on puts the damage centroid
  175 milli-cells away from where the oblique hit puts it, against a threshold of
  100.

### What this measured that is worth knowing

**One canonical strong hit cracks but does not yet liberate.** 3500 energy breaks
184 bonds and frees nothing: the cut is not closed. The *second* identical hit
breaks 605 and frees 190 cells in 15 pieces, the largest 60 cells — because
crack-tip concentration does the work of closing the surface over two hits. At
9000 energy a single hit frees 264 cells in 44 pieces.

The benchmark target was not moved to accommodate this. `strong_center_hit`
specifies one repetition and `fragment_creation: Allowed`, so it passes as
specified, and the committed results record `0` freed where a later refinement
should move it. Making one strong hit liberate on its own is break-character work,
which is 0006.5.

The second measured finding is a shape warning: a single 9000-energy hit produced
**44 components, several of them one to five cells**. Topology truth is topology
truth, but admitting forty-four bodies per hit is a fragment storm. The fragment
budget already has the final say through `detach_if`, and separating structural
chunks from cheap debris is 0006.6.

### The lab steps through it

Every implemented case is now executable from the replay lab (`F11`), using the
same pack-driven stimulus as the headless runner, so stepping through a case and
measuring it are the same hit. The dump gained the fracture content checksum and
a `last_separation` block — cells freed by cracks, pieces, cells freed by
deletion, inconclusive cross-checks, fragments admitted. Cases a later section
owns still block the script rather than being skipped, so a replay cannot look
complete while its most interesting step never ran.

### Acceptance

17 new focused tests, 49 in the crate's unit suite, and every pre-existing test
still green. `FractureState::digest()` was added for the deterministic result
checksum and is what 0006.2 will check a reloaded crack field against.

### What is deliberately not in this pass

- **Closing the cut in one hit.** Measured, recorded, and left to 0006.5.
- **Fragment hierarchy.** 44 one-to-five-cell pieces from one hit is recorded as a
  finding; deciding which are structural and which are debris is 0006.6.
- **Carrying a plug's cracks into the fragment it becomes.** The records are
  forgotten on detach, because remapping them needs fragment-local fracture
  space, which is 0006.3.
- **Region-granular fracture persistence.** 0006.2.

## 0006.2 — fracture-state ownership across streaming

**Status: complete**

Damage now survives world ownership moving. Region-granular extract and restore
in `engine_destruction`, a persistence sidecar in `engine_io`, two hygiene paths
for geometry that disappears by other routes, and the benchmark's
`previously_damaged_area` case made executable.

### Ownership follows identity

A cell record belongs to its cell's region. A bond record belongs to its **lower**
cell's region, which is already the bond's canonical name — so ownership needed no
second rule, and a test asserts every record is owned by exactly one region and
that the per-region counts sum to the whole state.

A bond whose upper cell lives in a *different* region therefore leaves memory with
the lower one. That is the conservative direction, not an accident: while the
record is away the face reads as intact, an intact bond carries support, and a
structure stays standing rather than falling because part of it was evicted. The
fracture-aware classifier already refuses to conclude anything across a
non-resident boundary, so the two rules agree.

A fragment's cracks are never owned by a static region. A fragment moves; its
damage cannot belong to whichever region its cells currently overlap.

### Extract, restore, and the checksum that proves it

| call | purpose |
|---|---|
| `static_regions()` | which regions hold damage, canonical order |
| `region_fracture(r)` | read a region's damage without removing it — for saving one that stays resident |
| `take_static_region(r)` | remove and return it — for eviction |
| `restore_region(records, limits)` | put it back, atomically |

`FractureState::digest()` is the proof. Taking every region leaves the state
empty and changes the checksum; restoring them all reproduces the original
checksum exactly — **and so does restoring them in reverse order**, because a
streamed world loads regions in whatever order the disk and the camera agree on.

A restore that would exceed an entry ceiling is refused whole, in both
dimensions, and a refused restore changes nothing. A record that means
"undamaged" — zero energy, or full integrity — is dropped rather than stored, so
the state's size depends on how much damage there is and not on how it was
written. Taking a region that holds nothing does not advance the revision.

### A sidecar, not a format change

Cracks live in `fracture/r.<x>.<y>.<z>.json`, beside the region shard, exactly as
fragments do. Three consequences, all wanted:

- `FORMAT_VERSION` does not move and no committed world fixture changed;
- a world with no `fracture/` directory loads with no damage and **no error**,
  which is the state every world written before this pass is in;
- a host that never opts into fracture pays nothing — not a field, not a file.

`engine_destruction` stays serde-free. The file shape is defined in `engine_io`
and converted at the boundary, the way `Fragment` already is, which is what stops
a file format from quietly becoming the in-memory layout.

Details that are decisions rather than details:

- A bond is written as its lower cell plus an axis — the canonical name of the
  face — so a file **cannot** express the same crack twice. `BondKey::along` is
  the only way back in, and a bond whose upper cell would leave the address space
  is dropped rather than trusted: a file is not permitted to create a key the
  engine itself would refuse to build.
- Saving a region with no damage **deletes** its sidecar. Writing an empty file
  would cost a file per region for nothing and leave a repaired region looking
  damaged-but-unmarked.
- A load that does not fit the host's entry budget is `IoError::FractureBudget`,
  not a parse error. The file is fine and the budget is not, and the message has
  to send a reader to the right place.
- The same cracks always write the same bytes; asserted by saving twice into
  different directories and comparing.

### Two hygiene paths, for two different situations

- `forget_removed(space, cells)` — the bounded path, driven by an edit's own
  `EditOutcome::removed_cells`. Cost is the number of cells that left.
- `prune_against(space, cells)` — the sweep, for after a bulk change whose extent
  a host did not track: a repaint, a region swap, a world reload. It drops every
  record that no longer describes the material it was recorded against, and every
  bond whose far cell has gone. Cost is the size of the **state**, never the size
  of the world, and `FracturePrune::records_examined` reports it so the sweep can
  be judged on evidence. A sweep over unchanged geometry drops nothing and does
  not advance the revision, and a static sweep leaves a fragment's cracks alone.

### `previously_damaged_area` runs now

The runner became staged: a case is a sequence of identical-hit groups, so a
precondition is applied **by the same code path** as the case's own stimulus
rather than by a second one that could drift away from it. Measurement starts at
the case's own stage, so a precondition's damage is never counted as the case's —
asserted by the damaged-area case walking exactly as many cells as one weak hit.

The precondition is keyed on the case rather than parsed out of the spec's prose
`precondition` field, because string-matching a sentence breaks in silence. A test

## 0006.3 — fragment-local fracture and true re-destruction

**Status: complete**

A detached chunk is now destructible by the same model that broke it loose, and it
arrives carrying the damage it was broken loose with.

### A plug's cracks are its cracks

Until this pass, a detached component's fracture records were *forgotten*. Every
fragment therefore arrived pristine however hard it had been hit — which threw
away real damage and made a chunk that had been pounded for six hits
indistinguishable from one that fell off clean.

`FractureState::adopt_into_fragment` moves them instead. Local coordinates are
`world - source_origin`, so records move without their geometry changing shape. A
bond with **one** cell in the fragment is neither moved nor kept: that face is
where the separation happened, so it is not a crack on either side afterwards — it
is a gap.

### Re-destruction is not a second path

`damage_fragment_store_if` already took its failures from anywhere, so the
fracture model feeds it directly: the only thing that was ever "scalar" about the
old path was where `failed` came from. Nothing about the reconcile transaction
changed.

What did change is that the **splitting rule became a parameter**.
`damage_fragment_store_with_parts` takes the function that decides what "one
object" means, and `damage_fragment_store_if` is now that same function with
`Fragment::connected_parts` — occupancy, unchanged, still the default and still
the oracle. A caller holding fracture state passes
`fragment_parts_through_cracks` instead, and a chunk that cracked in half comes
apart without a cell being deleted along the seam. Exactly what 0006.1 did for the
static world, in the space a fragment lives in.

There is still one atomic transaction. A refusing admission policy leaves the
store and the identity sequence untouched, and a deferred classification returns
the fragment whole rather than splitting an object along a budget.

One behavioural correction fell out of it: geometry can be *unchanged* and the
object still be in pieces, when the splitting rule is cracks rather than cells. So
`Unchanged` now means "nothing failed **and** it is still one object".

### Records follow cells through a split

`remap_fragment` retags a parent's records to the children that replaced them.
Children keep the parent's local frame — which is what makes re-fracture valid
after arbitrary backend rotation — so coordinates do not move and only the space
tag changes. A cell no child holds is dropped. A bond whose two cells landed in
**different** children is dropped too, for the same reason a separation bond is:
that is the seam, and a bond across it is a gap.

Tested both ways: damage away from the seam survives into the right child, and
exactly the seam bonds are dropped and nothing else.

### Fragment space is not world space

A fragment-local stimulus carries **no** `source_world` and **no**
`impulse_world`, and that is a decision rather than an omission. Those fields are
world-space by definition, a fragment's volume is not, and the engine stores
fragment rotation opaquely on purpose — so writing a local coordinate into a
world-space field would be a lie that happened to work until something rotated. A
caller hands the direction over through `FractureImpact::from_event`, which is
exactly the seam that API exists for.

`FractureScene::of_fragment` closes the other half: nothing in a fragment is
anchored, because having no foundation is what it means to have come loose, and a
fragment held in memory is entirely resident, so there is no unknown space inside
one. A test confirms the static world's anchored-cell exemption cannot quietly
protect a fragment cell from failing.

### `detached_chunk_hit` runs now

The precondition is honest about what it is: the committed strong-centre
stimulus, repeated up to a bounded four times until it liberates something, and
the largest piece it liberated. Nothing about the chunk is hand-authored, so a
change to the fracture model changes the chunk too — which is the point of
measuring against it. The ceiling exists so that a tuning change which stops
liberating fails the benchmark instead of looping.

The pack's target for a fragment case is read as an **offset from the chunk's
centre**, not an absolute local cell. A fragment's local coordinates depend on
where it broke off and are not knowable when the pack is written, which is exactly
why the committed target is `[0, 0, 0]`; under the absolute reading the hit misses
the object entirely and the case measures nothing, which is worse than leaving it
unimplemented. The target resolves to the occupied cell nearest that point, with
ties broken by canonical cell order, and the lab uses the same reading so stepping
through the case and measuring it are the same hit.

Measured, seven of eight cases executable:

| case | cells walked | broken bonds | failed cells | fragments |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `weak_center_hit` | 817 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `repeated_center_hits` (3x) | 2450 | 32 | 1 | 0 |
| `strong_center_hit` | 817 | 184 | 5 | 0 |
| `edge_hit` | 516 | 39 | 1 | 0 |
| `angled_hit` | 842 | 59 | 0 | 0 |
| `previously_damaged_area` | 817 | 28 | 1 | 0 |
| `detached_chunk_hit` | 60 | 0 | 54 | 4 |

The canonical chunk is **60 cells**. One 2500-energy hit fails 54 of them and
leaves 4 child fragments totalling 6 cells. A detached chunk is emphatically not
indestructible debris.

### What this measured that is worth knowing

**A small chunk is far more fragile than the wall it came from, and more than it
should be.** 54 of 60 cells in one hit is close to pulverisation. The cause is
structural, not a tuning accident: a 60-cell chunk is almost entirely surface, the
free-surface rule gives every exposed cell its spall bonus, and the bonuses
compound where the wall's buried interior damped them. The model is behaving
exactly as written; what it is written to do is wrong at this scale.

That is a break-character problem, so it is recorded here and left to 0006.5
rather than patched now. The committed results carry the number so the improvement
will be visible.

### The lab hits chunks too

A replay script asking for `detached_chunk_hit` hits the largest live chunk, using
the same centre-relative reading and the same reconcile transaction as the
headless runner, and reports whether it dented, broke apart or was destroyed. A
chunk the lab breaks off the wall now becomes a live fragment carrying its own
cracks, so hitting the wall and then hitting what fell off it is one continuous
sequence.

### Acceptance

12 new fragment tests, and every pre-existing test still green.

### What is deliberately not in this pass

- **Secondary destruction.** A falling chunk does not yet damage what it lands on.
  That is 0006.4 and the last unexecutable benchmark case.
- **Break character at chunk scale.** Measured, recorded, 0006.5.
- **Fragment-local fracture persistence.** The sidecar is region-keyed; a
  fragment's cracks live in memory with the fragment. Fragment shards already
  persist separately, so this is a sidecar shape question rather than a model one.

## Future, after review: 0006.4 — fragment impact coupling and secondary destruction

Falling debris joins the same destruction loop. A fragment-to-static impact feeds
bounded energy into fracture; fragment-to-fragment impacts become supported with
