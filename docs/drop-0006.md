# DROP 0006 — Core Destruction: the Fracture Model

Status: **in progress**

DROP 0006 is about one question, asked properly for the first time:

> what happens *inside* one solid material when one impact hits it?

Everything before this drop could answer only "some cells reached a threshold and
stopped existing". The pipeline that did it is correct, proven and staying — but
it has no state between *intact* and *absent*, so an impact could only ever
delete a shape, and the shape it deleted was the shape of the event. That is why
destruction still read as

```text
damage -> cells disappear -> connectivity -> clean fragment falls
```

even though every stage of it works.

This drop is not about making destruction prettier. It is about building the
**real fracture state** that later visuals, materials, fragments and collapse can
all derive from. A crack has to exist as engine data before it can be rendered,
persisted, streamed, re-broken or made to differ between wood and steel.

**One material, on purpose.** The whole drop develops against a single
homogeneous baseline solid. Material diversity is DROP 0005's subject and it is
deliberately switched off here: with five materials in the fixture, a
fracture-model problem and a tuning problem look identical.

> DROP 0005 is not finished — 0005.8, participation and fantasy acceptance,
> remains open, as does closing out the DROP 5 capture work. Taking the core
> fracture model first is a deliberate reordering, not an omission: the
> destruction model is what everything else in the drop ends up expressed in, and
> getting it wrong later is more expensive than getting 0005.8 late.

## 0006.0 — baseline fracture model and one impact

**Status: complete**

Adds `engine_destruction::fracture`: sparse persistent fracture state and a
deterministic reference impact evaluator. One homogeneous wall, one localised
impact, repeated — and the questions are where the energy went, what weakened,
what actually failed, and what is still standing.

### Where fracture sits, and why it is not anywhere else

Fracture **consumes `DamageEvent` directly, beside the scalar path.** Three
placements were on the table and two of them do not work:

- **After `DamageWork`** does not work. `DamageWork` is `{event, target, amount}`.
  By the time an event has become work the geometry is gone: no impact position,
  no direction, no distance. A model whose whole subject is *where the energy
  went* cannot be built on a representation that threw the geometry away.
- **Inside `ProgressiveDamageStore`** does not work either. That store is one
  scalar per cell and deletes its record at threshold, which is the right
  contract for accumulated applied damage and the wrong one for a crack. Merging
  them would also put one accumulator under two different threshold policies — a
  weapon hit and an impact are not the same question about a cell.
- **Beside, from the same event** works, because `DamageEvent` already carries
  `source_world`, `impulse_world` and the affected volume: exactly the three
  things a fracture model needs and the three things `DamageWork` discards.

```text
             DamageEvent
                  |
    +-------------+--------------+
    |                            |
 DamageWork                 FractureImpact
    |                            |
 ProgressiveDamageStore      FractureState   (cell energy + bond integrity)
    |                            |
    +-------------+--------------+
                  |
            failed cells
                  |
       WorldEditBatch -> connectivity -> Fragment
```

`DamageEvent`, `DamageWork` and `ProgressiveDamageStore` are **unchanged**. Not
one line of the DROP 0004 damage contract moved. A host may run both paths from
one event, or either alone, and neither knows the other exists.

### Bonds, and the invariant this does not touch

State is sparse and held two ways:

- per **cell**, accumulated absorbed energy, which destroys the cell outright at
  the profile's crush threshold;
- per **bond** — one face between two occupied cells — a remaining integrity that
  falls as the bond is loaded and reaches zero when it breaks.

A bond representation is right here because Micrology's structural topology is
*already* face-connected, so a crack has somewhere natural to live: two cells
stay present while the connection between them stops existing. That is the state
everything later wants — an irregular break surface, a weak region the next hit
concentrates into, a plug that is loose before it is gone.

**Occupancy connectivity remains the only topological oracle.** `classify_from_roots`
is untouched and means exactly what it did. A broken bond is a crack, not a cut,
and nothing in the fracture module detaches anything. Fracture-aware
connectivity — treating a broken bond as a cut when deciding what is held up — is
0006.1, and when it arrives it sits *beside* the exact occupancy classifier
rather than replacing it. Weakening one of the strongest invariants in the engine
to make cracking easier would be a bad trade at any price.

In this pass a bond breaking reaches geometry by exactly one route: **a cell all
of whose bonds have broken is held by nothing, and fails.** That failure is an
ordinary `DamageTarget`, so it flows through `static_failure_batch`,
`WorldEditBatch`, connectivity and the fragment pipeline without any of them
learning a new concept.

### A bond is named once

`BondKey` stores the **lower** cell plus the axis, with private fields and
normalising constructors. The obvious `(cell, FaceDir)` key names every face
twice, and two aliases of one crack accumulate damage separately — so a bond
would break at twice the load its capacity says. Derived ordering is
`(lower, axis)`, which is what makes `BTreeMap` output canonical.

### The model

Six numbers describe the baseline solid, and each has exactly one job:

| field | job |
|---|---|
| `crush` | accumulated absorbed energy that destroys a cell |
| `tensile` | bond capacity pulled apart — lowest |
| `compressive` | bond capacity driven together — highest |
| `shear` | bond capacity across the face |
| `toughness_milli` | share of local energy soaked up rather than turned into cracks |
| `density_milli` | mass of one cell; carried as authored data, read by nothing here |

Toughness is used **once**, as the complement of brittleness in bond loading.
Using it again anywhere would make the material quietly twice as durable as its
numbers say — the same mistake `engine_mechanics` records for toughness in
applied damage, in the same shape. Density is deliberately inert: fragment mass
already has its own reference policy, and giving the number a second job would be
two models of one quantity.

An impact deposits energy, and bonds are loaded by the **gradient** of that
deposit:

1. **Walk, do not sample.** The propagation is a bounded face walk over occupied
   cells from a small seed box around the impact origin. Walking is what puts
   local geometry in the answer: energy cannot cross a void, and a cell reachable
   only by a long detour is out of range even when it is close. Attenuation is by
   straight-line distance, because attenuating by face-walk distance makes a
   solid's damage field octahedral — graph distance in a solid *is* Manhattan
   distance, and it shows.
2. **Direction matters.** A cell's deposit is raised up to 1.6x facing the
   impulse and cut to 0.4x facing away. This is the single thing that stops the
   result being a sphere.
3. **Free surfaces spall.** A cell with an exposed face has nowhere to pass the
   energy on, so it keeps what would have travelled onward. This is why the back
   of a struck wall breaks out rather than only the face that was hit, and it is
   the main source of an irregular break surface rather than a clean shell.
4. **A bond is loaded by the difference across it.** Uniform energy on both sides
   moves two cells together; only a gradient drives them apart. So the biggest
   loads land at the *edge* of the damaged region rather than at its hottest
   point, which is why a plug comes loose instead of a sphere vanishing.
5. **Mode comes from geometry, not from authored data.** The cell the wave
   reaches first is upstream. Upstream absorbing more means it is driven into the
   one ahead: compression. Downstream absorbing more means it is running away:
   tension. Capacity is blended between the axial mode and shear by how well the
   bond's axis aligns with the impulse, so there is no threshold cliff. One rule,
   and it makes the struck face crush while the back face — raised by the
   free-surface bonus — tears off in tension, where the material is weakest.
6. **Damage concentrates at a crack tip.** A bond beside already-broken bonds
   carries more of the next hit, capped so that a bond surrounded by damage
   cannot break to an arbitrarily small load. This is what makes a repeated hit
   *grow* the existing damage instead of re-damaging a fresh ring, and it is the
   evolving weak region the design asked for.
7. **There is a fatigue floor.** A load below 2% of bond integrity is not
   recorded. Two things come out of that and both are wanted: the state stays
   proportional to the damage rather than to the reach, and an arbitrarily weak
   hit repeated forever never breaks anything — which is what real materials do.

### Determinism

The model is integer. `DamageEvent` carries `f64` world coordinates, so there is
exactly **one** quantisation step — `FractureImpact::from_event` — and everything
downstream is `i64`/`i128` with saturating conversions. Cell centres never go
through floating point at all: a cell address is an `i32`, so its centre in
milli-cells is `x * 1000 + 500` exactly. Output is canonicalised through
`BTreeMap`, so traversal order is not observable, and the deltas come out
ascending by cell and by bond key.

`Milli` was not reused. It lives in `engine_mechanics`, which is *downstream* of
`engine_destruction`, and fracture state has to be storable beside fragments
whether or not a world opted into mechanics.

### Conservative under everything

- **Unknown is not empty.** A non-resident neighbour *within the impact's reach*
  yields `Indeterminate` and the regions needed. Beyond the reach it cannot
  change the answer, so it is not reported — being conservative about the unknown
  is not the same as being paralysed by it, and reporting every region boundary
  would defer every impact near one for nothing.
- **A spent budget is not destruction.** Work ceilings yield `Deferred`, which
  carries no deltas and can mutate nothing.
- **Memory ceilings refuse the whole transaction.** There is no half-applied
  fracture state, and a refusal does not advance the revision.
- **Work and byte budgets are separate**, as everywhere else: cells walked and
  bonds loaded are the work bounds; cell entries and bond entries are the memory
  bounds.
- **Async results are stale-checked.** An evaluation carries the state `Revision`
  it was computed against and is refused if the state has moved, so a result
  computed on a worker thread cannot be committed onto state that changed under
  it.
- **An anchored cell is never failed by losing its bonds.** Losing bonds is a
  structural inference, and an anchor is the author's statement that something is
  held up regardless — the same rule 0005.6 arrived at. It can still be crushed,
  because that is applied damage and applied damage has never respected anchors.
- **Stale state is forgotten, not inherited.** `forget_cell` drops a cell's
  record and every bond touching it, for when geometry disappears by a path
  fracture did not drive. A bond to a cell that is gone is a hole, not a crack.

### One material, by construction

`BaselineFracturePolicy` applies one profile to every material because it holds
one profile and no table. Material diversity is not merely unused here, it is
**unexpressible**. Making fracture material-aware later is the move DROP 0005
already made for damage: a new `FracturePolicy` implementation in
`engine_mechanics`, with nothing in this module changing.

### The permanent fixture: the Micrology destruction wall

32 cells wide, 18 tall, 5 thick, on an anchored footing wider than itself, one
material throughout. Exact dimensions are not sacred; large enough to form
meaningful chunks and small enough to read at a glance is.

### What the model actually does

The same hit on the same spot, seven times, energy 900, reach 7:

| hit | cracks opened | cells removed | damaged cells | loaded bonds | broken bonds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 0 | 497 | 1840 | 0 |
| 2 | 4 | 1 | 496 | 1835 | 4 |
| 3 | 28 | 0 | 496 | 1835 | 32 |
| 4 | 184 | 5 | 491 | 1814 | 208 |
| 5 | 308 | 4 | 487 | 1814 | 508 |
| 6 | 201 | 33 | 454 | 1721 | 621 |
| 7 | 180 | 21 | 433 | 1652 | 756 |

The first hit leaves the wall standing and measurably weaker, which is the thing
the old pipeline could not express. Seven hits take 64 of 3132 cells — about 2% —
and what they take is not a sphere:

```text
struck face (z=2)              mid-wall (z=1, z=0)       back (z=-1)
##############.###.#########   ###############...####    #########.####
#############..###..########   ###############...####    ##############
############.........#######   ###############...####    ##############
##############.....#########
############.........#######
#############..###..########
##############.###.#########
```

A ragged, lobed crater at the face it was hit on, narrowing to a 3x3 channel
through the middle, with a single cell punched out one layer short of
perforating. The lobes are the free-surface rule doing its work; a spherical mask
has no reason to produce them.

The crack field reaches considerably further than the hole: **hole half-width 4
cells, crack half-width 7** — so roughly three times the area of the wall is
present-and-weakened as is gone, and the next hit anywhere inside that will find
it.

Cost for one hit of that size: 817 cells walked, 1960 bonds loaded. Both are
bounded and both are reported; neither is presented as a frame-rate claim.

`cargo run -p engine_destruction --example fracture_wall` prints this table, the
spreads and every slice through the wall, so none of it has to be taken on trust
— and it needs no GPU.

### Acceptance

24 / 24 focused tests, including:

- **Test A** — one weak hit damages the wall without removing a single cell, and
  what it leaves is not a spherical mask: a cell downstream of the impulse
  absorbs strictly more than one the same distance upstream.
- **Test B** — repeating the same hit builds on the previous state rather than
  resetting it, opens the first cracks on the second hit, never un-breaks a
  crack, and eventually produces local failure.
- cracks reach further than the hole;
- the struck face is compressed while the far side is pulled apart;
- the same sequence replays to identical state and identical surviving geometry;
- a stale evaluation, an exhausted work budget and an exhausted entry budget each
  change nothing;
- unknown space within reach is indeterminate, and unknown space beyond it is not;
- energy cannot cross a void to reach a separate slab;
- an anchored cell is never failed by losing its bonds;
- a hit below the fatigue floor never breaks anything however often it repeats.

Every existing test still passes: the full `cargo test` suite is green and
nothing outside the new module and its registration changed in any engine crate.

### The diagnostic

`MICROLOGY_FRACTURE_DEMO=1` replaces the scene with the destruction wall, fixes
the camera square on to the struck face, holds the intact wall, then fires the
same impact seven times and holds the result.

The overlay draws the fracture state itself, not an impression of it: a
cross-marked red quad on every face whose bond has broken, an amber cross in
every cell past a quarter of its crush threshold sized and coloured by how close
it is, and a white marker at the impact origin. It draws with a negative depth
bias on purpose — fracture state lives *inside* a solid wall, and a diagnostic
that respected depth would show almost none of it. `H` hides the overlay and the
text together, for capture. The status block reports the real sparse-state
counters, the work the last impact cost, and how many impacts were held by a
budget or by unknown space.

No particles. Nothing in the picture exists that is not in `FractureState`.

### What is deliberately not in this pass

- **Fracture-aware connectivity.** Broken bonds do not yet decide what is held
  up, so a region the cracks have genuinely separated still hangs together until
  cells are removed. This is the single biggest thing 0006.0 leaves on the table
  and it is 0006.1.
- **Region-granular persistence.** `forget_cell` and `forget_space` keep the
  state honest when geometry leaves, but there is no `take_static_region` /
  restore pair yet, so fracture state does not travel with a streamed region.
- **Fragment-local fracture.** The API is space-aware throughout — a
  `FractureScene` can be a fragment — but DROP 0004.8's fragment re-damage path
  still runs on the scalar model, and folding it into this one is a later
  section.
- **Material diversity, LOD, chain collapse, visual crack geometry.** Not now,
  and not by accident.

## Next: 0006.1 — fracture-aware connectivity, beside the oracle

Make a broken bond count as a cut when deciding what is still held up, as an
additional connectivity source rather than a change to the existing one. The
exact occupancy classifier stays exactly as it is and stays the permanent
topological oracle; the fracture-aware classifier is tested *against* it, in the
same relationship `GreedyCompiler` has with `ExactCompiler`. A structure whose
fracture-aware answer differs from its occupancy answer is where a plug comes
loose without a single cell being deleted — which is the point of having bonds at
all.
