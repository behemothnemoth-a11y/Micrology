## What was added

`crates/engine_mechanics/tests/participation_fantasy.rs` drives the real
material damage, exact connectivity and structural-capacity APIs through six
policy arrangements.

No production engine code changed.

### 1. Full mechanics

Every `PhysicalSystem` is enabled.

The fixture proves all three engine questions remain independent and available:

- reference glass fails to the material-aware light hit;
- an overloaded anchored masonry wall produces mechanical failures;
- an unanchored masonry island is classified detached when connectivity
  detachment is requested.

### 2. Damage only

Only `AppliedDamage` participates.

The same light hit is still accumulated through the upstream DROP 0004 uniform
damage contract because `MaterialMechanics` is disabled. Structural capacity is
`NotEvaluated`, and connectivity detachment is not requested.

Turning material mechanics off therefore does not silently turn damage off.

### 3. Connectivity only

Only `ConnectivityDetachment` participates.

The exact connectivity oracle identifies the complete unanchored island as
detached while applied damage is not invoked and structural capacity returns
`NotEvaluated`.

Connectivity remains a separate physical policy, not a side effect of mechanics.

### 4. Silent / mechanics-disabled world

A completely silent `ParticipationPolicy` is run against an overloaded world
made from fully profiled masonry.

Nothing participates:

- no applied damage;
- no connectivity detachment;
- no capacity analysis;
- no cell changes.

The material profile does not make physics mandatory.

### 5. Floating-island object override

The **world enables every physical system**. A more-specific object policy then
disables:

```text
gravity
connectivity detachment
fragment physics
structural capacity
```

while deliberately inheriting:

```text
applied damage
material mechanics
```

The unanchored profiled masonry island accepts a tiny real material-aware damage
record, but neither detachment nor capacity is run and its authored geometry
remains unchanged.

This is the fantasy contract in the form it was designed for: an impossible
object can still be damageable without pretending to be infinitely strong or
being forced to obey continuous structural simulation.

### 6. Adjacent regions with different participation

Two overloaded walls live in neighboring residency regions in one `World`.

Both are made from the **same MASONRY MaterialId and the same mechanical
registry**.

The host selects the normal world policy for one region/structure and a
more-specific object/region policy disabling material mechanics and structural
capacity for the adjacent one.

Result:

```text
participating masonry wall  → MechanicalFailures::Failed
fantasy-policy masonry wall → Held(NotEvaluated)
```

There is no magic material and no infinite-capacity profile. The difference is
policy.

This also records the intended boundary: `ParticipationPolicy` is selected by
the host for a world/region/structure/object analysis. Capacity itself does not
become a per-cell spatial-policy authority.

composition.
